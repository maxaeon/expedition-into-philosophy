# Modern European Philosophy

The region map for the modern period, drawn on the same traced geography as the world map's west view, with a back button to the world map. Its header states the organizing question plainly, where knowledge comes from, and names the two roads through it, the rationalist answer that reason working from ideas the mind brings with it can establish truths about reality, and the empiricist answer that every material of knowledge arrives through the senses. The stops run in order and jump decades, and each will state its real time and place.

Planned stops, all in preparation. Descartes and the Stove-Heated Room, the doubt, the one survivor of the doubt, and the letters of Elisabeth of Bohemia as the objection beat, in the role Gaunilo's letter plays in the Monastery. Hume in Edinburgh, impressions and ideas, the missing shade of blue, and custom carrying every expectation about tomorrow. Kant in Königsberg, the attempted settlement between the two roads. Wollstonecraft in London, the era's confidence in reason applied to the era's own arrangements. Completion keys will be `phil_map_descartes`, `phil_map_hume`, `phil_map_kant`, and `phil_map_wollstonecraft`, and completing all four will complete the region, which together with the Monastery opens The Atlantic World.
